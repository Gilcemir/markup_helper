# ADR-002: A broken contributor name warns but never blocks CRediT injection

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-14

## Context

Three v26n3 articles reach Phase 3 with a `<contrib>` whose `<surname>` is
empty or carries an ORCID or affiliation digit instead of a name (5316: seven
authors; 5613: one; 5501: one). Today nothing reports this; the operator only
notices when the initials fail to resolve, and even then the report blames the
CREDIT statement. A new verification rule (`contrib-names`) will detect these
shapes. The question is what the CRediT injector should do with such a
contributor.

In 5316, three of the seven broken contributors still resolve because the
author's initials happen to match the full name kept in `<given-names>`. The
roles land on the right `<contrib>`; only the surname element is wrong.

## Decision

`contrib-names` emits a WARN per affected contributor and changes nothing
else. The `credit-roles` rule keeps its current behavior: a contributor whose
initials resolve uniquely receives its roles, whether or not its name elements
are well-formed. The WARN and the CRediT pendency are reported separately so
the operator can tell "fix the name in Markup" from "resolve the initials".

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Warn and exclude the contributor from resolution

- **Description**: A contributor flagged by `contrib-names` is removed from the
  resolver's candidate list; its author key falls to the prompt.
- **Pros**: Never attaches roles to an element whose name is known to be wrong.
- **Cons**: Turns correct injections (PSA, ATB, RRP in 5316) into manual work
  for no gain: the roles were on the right contributor; the surname is fixed in
  Markup independently of CRediT.
- **Why rejected**: Adds pendencies without preventing any wrong injection.

### Alternative 2: Warn and block the whole article

- **Description**: No CRediT is injected while any contributor name is broken.
- **Pros**: Maximum internal consistency of the output XML.
- **Cons**: Couples two unrelated fixes; the operator loses the auto-applied
  roles of every other author in the article.
- **Why rejected**: Disproportionate; the name defect and the CRediT mapping
  are independent editorial tasks.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Broken names become visible on every article, including those without a
  CREDIT statement.
- Auto-application rate is unaffected by name defects that the resolver can
  still see through.

### Negative

- The output XML may carry `<role>` elements inside a `<contrib>` whose
  `<surname>` is still wrong until the operator fixes it in Markup. The WARN is
  the only guard.

### Risks

- Operator ignores the WARN and the broken surname reaches production XML.
  Mitigation: the batch summary lists broken-name counts per article as a
  release-blocking pendency for the edition.

## Implementation Notes

The rule is verification-only: it does not write to the XML. Detection is by
shape of `<surname>` text (empty, contains digits, matches the ORCID pattern).
