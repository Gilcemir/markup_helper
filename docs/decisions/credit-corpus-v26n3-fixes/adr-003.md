# ADR-003: Widen the author-keyed CREDIT grammar with shape-based lookback, and never drop a present statement

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-14

## Context

`CreditStatementParser.TryParseAuthorKeyed` splits entries on `.` and terms on
`,`; `;` is accepted only between keys before the `:`. The CBAB v26n3 corpus
uses `;` between terms (5642, 5412, 5528, 5441, 5501) and between author
entries (5547, 5613, 5501), which sends three structured statements to
`Prose` and contaminates five others with junk terms. Published CRediT
examples (Wiley, Elsevier, CASRAI) also use `;` between roles, so this is the
mainstream convention, not a deviation.

Widening the separators creates one ambiguity: in
`Conceptualization; CFA; MN; MDVR; CDC: Methodology` the pieces `CFA`, `MN`,
`MDVR` carry no `:` yet are keys of the next entry, not terms of the previous
one.

Separately, `DocxSourceReader.ExtractSection` ends the section at any piece
starting with `[`, which swallows a body paragraph tagged `[p]…[/p]` (5536)
and reports the statement as absent at INFO level.

## Decision

1. Entries are still split on `.`; inside an entry, the text after the first
   `:` is split on `;` and `,`. A piece containing `:` opens a new entry. A
   piece without `:` that is an initials block (two or more letters, all
   uppercase, the same predicate the resolver uses) is held as a pending key
   and attached to the next entry opened by a `:` piece. Every other piece is
   a term of the current entry.
2. The statement falls back to `Prose` when any `.`-segment lacks a `:`, when
   an entry ends with no term, when pending keys are never closed by a `:`
   piece, or when no term maps to a CRediT term. Keys before the first `:` are
   split on `;` and `,`.
3. `ExtractSection` strips `[p]` and `[/p]` (case-insensitive) from each piece
   before the bracket test; any other bracket tag or a `REFERENCES` heading
   still ends the section. `DocxSource` records that the CREDIT STATEMENT
   header was found so the injector can WARN when the body is empty.

**INV-02 — A present CREDIT statement is never silently dropped.** When the
docx carries a `CREDIT STATEMENT` header, Phase 3 either parses its body or
reports it at WARN level or above. An INFO "no statement" line is emitted only
when the header itself is absent.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Lookback by term mapping

- **Description**: pieces before a `:` piece that fail `CreditTermTable.TryMap`
  become co-keys.
- **Pros**: also admits keys such as `Lopes DAPS`.
- **Cons**: a misspelled term (`Metodology`) becomes a phantom key, resolves
  to `NotFound`, and the prompt blames the wrong thing.
- **Why rejected**: the corpus keys are initials blocks; shape is a stricter,
  explainable predicate with no collision against the 14 CRediT terms.

### Alternative 2: Reject mixed runs

- **Description**: a run mixing terms and colon-less pieces before a `:`
  falls to `Prose`.
- **Pros**: no heuristic.
- **Cons**: 5501 stays prose; fails the acceptance table.
- **Why rejected**: leaves the most common multi-author layout unparsed.

### Alternative 3: Normalize separators before parsing

- **Description**: rewrite `;` to `,` inside the term list before the existing
  parser runs.
- **Pros**: no parser change.
- **Cons**: cannot tell `term; KEY; KEY: term` from `term; term`; the
  ambiguity is structural, not lexical.
- **Why rejected**: does not solve the 5501 shape.

## Consequences

### Positive

- 8 of 15 v26n3 statements parse; the "free prose" label becomes accurate.
- 5536's statement is read; an emptied section is visible.

### Negative

- The grammar is more permissive; a future all-uppercase non-key token would
  be misread as a key (mitigated: it then resolves `NotFound` and prompts, and
  the raw text is in the diagnostic).

### Risks

- Regression on role-keyed statements: none expected, role-keyed runs first
  and is unchanged; existing fixtures stay as tests.

## Implementation Notes

`AuthorInitialsResolver.IsInitialsToken` becomes `internal static` and is
reused by the parser. `[p]` stripping is applied in `ExtractSection` for both
sections (DATA AVAILABILITY benefits too).
